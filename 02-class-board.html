The Class Board แผงวรรณยุกต์

Thai spells a tone with three things: the class of the consonant, the shape of the syllable, and a mark if there is one. This page sorts all 44 consonants into the three classes and lets you try the rule.

Learner Someone who can already see the letters and wants the reason a word rises or falls.
A card holds The letter, the letters with the same sound, and the tone of the shape you chose.
Hear it The pairs that share a spelling pattern are in the Listening Room.

ข and ค are the same puff of air. ส and ซ are the same s. Class is the part that changes the tone. Pick a letter, then a situation. The pattern written on that letter is a shape, and it may not be a dictionary word. A real word with the same class and the same situation sits next to it.

The same rule, joined to real words and to the vowel seats, is the combined workshop. English and Traditional Chinese. The colours there keep one meaning each.

  1. 1Pick a letter on the board. Its class colour stays with it.
  2. 2Pick a situation beside the inspector: live, dead, or a tone mark.
  3. 3Read the tone. Click any cell in the chart below to try that class and shape.
The rule for an ordinary syllable

A live syllable has a long vowel, or it ends in ม น ง ย ว, or it uses ไ ใ เ–า or ำ. A dead syllable ends in a short vowel, or in a stopped ก ด บ. A tone mark overrides that live-or-dead split. ไม้ตรี and ไม้จัตวา are written on mid-class consonants.

Still outside this calculator: a silent ห in front of ง ญ น ม ย ร ล ว, which makes the syllable follow high-class rules, as in หนู, หญิง, and แหวน. A pronounced cluster such as ปลา takes the class of its first consonant. ก็ is irregular. Final letters also change sound: several of them are pronounced น, and the stopped endings collapse to p, t, or k. Those belong on a later board.
